How to dock

You fly a 12-tonne crew capsule against a space station in a 420 km circular orbit. Motion is computed in the station's local frame with the Clohessy–Wiltshire (Hill) equations, so orbital mechanics is real: thrust toward the station along the velocity direction (V-bar) and you drift up and fall behind; approach from below (R-bar) and gravity gradient brakes you for free.

Capture limits (soft-capture ring): closing speed 0.01–0.15 m/s, lateral miss ≤ 0.15 m, misalignment ≤ 5°, roll ≤ 5°, body rates ≤ 1°/s. Hit the ring outside them and it's a collision.

Rule of thumb from real ops: keep the closing rate below about range ÷ 100 per second inside 100 m (the panel shows it in green/amber/red). Use the docking camera and put the standoff cross in the centre of the target circle.

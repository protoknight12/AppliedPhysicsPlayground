    Getting to orbit

    A two-stage kerosene/LOX launcher of Falcon 9 proportions: nine 845 kN engines on the first stage (282 s at sea level, 311 s in vacuum), one 981 kN vacuum engine (348 s) on the second. The air is the US Standard Atmosphere 1976, drag uses a Mach-dependent coefficient with the transonic peak, the Earth rotates (the pad already moves east at ~400 m/s at Cape Canaveral) and the orbit shown is computed from the real state vector.

    Limits

    Aerodynamic load q·α ≤ 150 kPa·°, acceleration ≤ 6 g, fairing off only when q < 1 kPa. Stage with the first-stage engines shut down.

    How it's done: rise vertically, kick over a few degrees toward the launch azimuth at ~60 m/s, then hold prograde and let gravity turn you. Throttle back through max-Q (~30 kPa near Mach 1.3), stage at depletion, and on the second stage pitch so the climb rate dies away as you reach orbital speed — the yellow flight-director cue shows the pitch the guidance would fly.

    Earth: NASA Blue Marble & Black Marble with a single-scattering atmosphere. Vehicle numbers are representative, not a specific mission.

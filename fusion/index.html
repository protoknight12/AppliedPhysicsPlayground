Event log

    How to run a shot, and what the model does

    Start-up. Start the vacuum pumps, cryoplant and primary coolant. When the coils are below 5 K, energise the TF magnets and charge the central solenoid. Prefill D-T gas (≈0.5–3 mPa; wider with ECRH assist) and initiate breakdown.

    Ramp-up. Raise Ip. Above ~4 MA the plasma forms an X-point and becomes diverted. Add heating until Psep beats the L–H threshold, then raise density with pellets and seed neon to keep divertor heat flux under 10 MW/m².

    Ending. Use Soft stop before the solenoid flux runs out. SPI (shattered-pellet injection) is the emergency button.

    Physics. A 0D power balance with 1D profiles: Bosch–Hale D-T reactivity, IPB98(y,2) confinement, Martin L–H threshold, Spitzer resistivity, bootstrap and driven current, bremsstrahlung and impurity line radiation, helium ash, Greenwald, Troyon and q95 limits.

    Events. ELMs (smaller and faster with RMP coils or pellet pacing), vertical instability with a saturating feedback loop, stochastic disruptions with thermal and current quench, runaway-electron beams, magnet quench and first-wall overheating.

    Time. Plant start-up (normally weeks) is compressed to about a minute; plasma physics runs in real seconds scaled by the speed buttons.

    Fuels, magnets, blankets. D–D, D–³He and p–¹¹B use their own Bosch–Hale (p–¹¹B: tabulated) reactivities, energy per reaction, charged-particle fraction, ash and Zeff — so you can see why they need far hotter, better-confined plasmas. HTS magnets reach 12 T at 20 K with a smaller cryoplant (gyrotrons are retuned to the field). The blanket picks the coolant, the steam conditions and the tritium breeding ratio; the plant overview shows the whole loop to the cooling tower.

    Things to try. Push n̄e past Greenwald with mitigation off. Drop BT to 3.6 T at 15 MA and watch q95. Raise κ to 1.95 with RMP off and see ELM kicks overwhelm vertical control. Turn the neon off and watch the divertor. Beat the autopilot's Q.

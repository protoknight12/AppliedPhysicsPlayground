Flying the lander

An Apollo-class lunar module: about 7 t dry, one throttleable descent engine (45 kN, Isp 311 s, minimum 10 % throttle) and RCS jets for attitude. Gravity is the Moon's 1.62 m/s², there's no air, and the surface curves away at the real lunar radius — from high up the horizon is tens of kilometres away.

Touchdown limits: vertical ≤ 2 m/s, horizontal ≤ 1.2 m/s, tilt ≤ 10°, ground slope ≤ 10°. Up to 3 m/s / 2 m/s / 15° is a hard landing; beyond that, the gear fails. The contact light comes on when the 1.7 m probes under the footpads touch — cut the engine then, like the real crews did.

Fuel callouts are hover-time remaining: “60 seconds”, “30 seconds”. Tilting costs vertical thrust: lift = thrust × cos(tilt).

Imagery: Moon from NASA's CGI Moon Kit (LRO LROC colour mosaic, Mare Tranquillitatis region on the horizon); Earth from NASA Blue Marble. Near terrain is procedurally generated with a realistic crater size distribution.

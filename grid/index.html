    How the grid works, and what the model does

    The job. Generation must equal demand every second. Any mismatch goes into (or out of) the kinetic energy of every spinning generator, and the frequency moves: 2H·S/f₀ · df/dt = Pgen − Pload. Keep it inside 49.8–50.2 Hz.

    Three layers of control. Inertia (H, seconds) resists the change instantly — nuclear, coal, gas and hydro add it, wind and solar don't. Primary response: governors in frequency response add 1/R × Δf/f₀ of their rating within seconds (droop R = 4 %). Secondary control (AGC) slowly moves set-points to bring 50.00 Hz back and re-balance.

    Why low inertia is hard. At sunny midday, with solar displacing thermal plant, the same 1.2 GW trip gives a much steeper RoCoF. Above 1 Hz/s the loss-of-mains relays on rooftop solar trip — losing even more generation. Batteries with fast frequency response or grid-forming inverters fix it.

    Dispatch. Each fleet has a minimum stable output, a ramp rate and a start-up time. Nuclear is cheap and inflexible, open-cycle gas is fast and expensive, hydro is fast but energy-limited, batteries and pumped storage have to be charged.

    Things to try. Turn off AGC and auto-dispatch and run the evening ramp by hand. In the duck-curve scenario, trip the nuclear unit at 13:00 with FFR off, then again with it on. In the winter peak, catch the TV pickup at 20:15. Watch what the storm does to an all-wind afternoon.

    Keys. Space pause · 1–4 speed · T trip largest unit.
